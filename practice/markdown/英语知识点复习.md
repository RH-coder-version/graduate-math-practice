Text Analysis
The text is a popular science article which interprets scientific findings for a general audience. Writers such as professional science journalists or scientists themselves publish popular science articles in newspapers and magazines to inform the general audience about new scientific findings. Normally, they do a few things to make their articles appealing and comprehensible to the readers. As the intended readers are not experts, it becomes extremely important to make articles easy and fun to read so as to maintain readers’ interest.
The following are the features of this text:
1) Subheadings are used to organize the structure well. Para. 1 is an introductory paragraph. Paras. 2 – 8 divide the text into seven independent sections, each responding to one fundamental question about climate change. These questions also function as subheadings of the text. The structure of this text improves readers’ reading efficiency by helping them better understand the main ideas and locate specific information.
2) Jargon and long sentences are kept to a minimum to simplify the content. The text only uses a few scientific terms. Terms like carbon dioxide, water vapor, etc. are relatively familiar to the general audience. In addition, the author avoids using excessively long sentences. The average length of the sentences is shorter than that of academic papers. Sentence structures are pretty basic in the text without excessive use of embedded clauses.
3) Concrete ideas are used to make arguments more convincing and persuasive. The author cites concrete evidence and statistics from scientific research. The concrete evidence and statistics help readers better make sense of the changes in temperature (Para. 2), sea levels (Para. 5), and polar ice caps (Para. 6), etc. In addition, concrete evidence increases the persuasiveness of this text.

Academic Exploration 2
Text Analysis
This text is a problem-and-solution essay. It briefly introduces the water scarcity problems we are facing and discusses possible solutions to water shortage problems in detail. The text can be divided into three main parts.
The first part (Para. 1) provides background information of global water shortage and poses the central question “So, what solutions are available to ensure a consistent and stable fresh water supply?” The second part (Paras.2−5) describes four main solutions to water shortage problems, namely changing dietary habits, desalination, cloud seeding, and reducing water consumption in the manufacturing sector. The last part (Para. 6) is the conclusion part. In this part, the author re-emphasizes the importance and urgency of taking action against water scarcity. 
Having clear topic sentences, especially in the main body paragraphs, is a noticeable feature of the text. These topic sentences can be identified at the beginning of each paragraph. For example, the first two sentences in Para. 2 highlight the significant impact of cutting consumption of animal products. The rest of this paragraph develops around the idea that cutting consumption of animal products can dramatically reduce the amount of water consumed by agriculture. Another example can be found in Para. 5, where the author states in the first sentence that “One final solution is to reduce water consumption in the manufacturing sector,” highlighting several key words in this paragraph, e.g. “manufacturing sector.” Using clear topic sentences helps readers to grasp the main idea of each paragraph without too much effort, which further facilitates their reading process.
Another feature of the text is the author’s objective tone. The author approaches each solution from two perspectives—its advantages and disadvantages. For example, in Para. 4, while acknowledging that cloud seeding provides an inexpensive and energy-efficient solution to water shortage problems, the author also cites its opponents by saying that people “cannot use the technique to actually generate clouds.” By discussing both sides of each solution, the author makes his / her tone objective, thus making readers more willing to accept ideas in the text.

Interpreting the Text
1 Although most of our planet is covered by water, only 3% of it is drinkable fresh water. Of the fresh water, 99% is inaccessible as it is buried beneath the world’s glaciers. As the world’s population is growing rapidly, coupled with the growth of water consumption in agriculture and manufacturing, the fresh water supply has reached an unsustainable level. Thus, the world’s population is still facing water scarcity.
2 It is true that switching to a vegetarian or vegan diet can reduce water consumption, as the production of meat is much more water-intensive than the production of vegetables. However, asking people to consume less (or no) meat means asking them to significantly change their dietary habits or even culture. As most of the population in almost all countries are not vegetarians, the feasibility of switching dietary habits is doubtful.
3 Desalination cannot be applied widely to address water shortage issues, at least not right now. First, desalination is a capital-intensive process, meaning that it costs lots of money. When a place cannot afford it financially, even if this place is located in coastal areas, desalination is not a viable option. Second, desalination is fairly inefficient. The water produced by 18,426 desalination plants around the world can only meet the needs of 1% of the world’s population. Therefore, desalination can only be applied in certain areas where there is a balance between investments and rewards.

Recycling more products means that few new products will be produced, and thus less water will be consumed.

Step 1: The model presents four crucial steps for personal and professional growth and development:
Comfort zone: It refers to a psychological state of being at ease and not feeling threatened. Within the comfort zone, people are not motivated since there isn’t much incentive for people to reach new heights of performance.
Fear zone: After people step out of the comfort zone, they may face new difficulties which they might have never dealt with. A lack of experience may lead to low self-confidence, and people tend to find excuses for their possible failure. A fear of failure or a fear of being incapable is what pushes most people back to the comfort zone. This can be anxiety-provoking. Yet if people keep on trying and persevere long enough, they will enter the learning zone.
Learning zone: People learn to deal with challenges and problems by acquiring new skills. This prepares them to accept more complex challenges.
Growth zone: After a period of learning, people live out their original dreams and are to achieve new greater heights.
It should be noted that after people leave the comfort zone, fear doesn’t always equate to being panic. Fear can be a necessary step en route to the learning and growth zone.
